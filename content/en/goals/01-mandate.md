# Mandate Alignment

[Back to home](../README.md)

**Mandate Alignment turns user intent into an authorized mandate that can be put into practice and maintained over time.** The mandate contains two kinds of commitment: delivery conditions specify what to deliver, how it will be accepted, and what evidence is needed; execution boundaries specify permitted actions, resource limits, delegated work, and decisions reserved for authorized parties. Interactive negotiation establishes these commitments, commitment operationalization connects them to checks and controls, and continuous maintenance keeps them valid as conditions change.

A request to “buy some computers for the department” does not yet specify the needs, available resources, or completion conditions. The governance agent should mediate between the user and the agentic system, actively establish the conditions on both sides, identify ambiguities, tradeoffs, and authorizations that require decisions, and assign decision authority according to the agreed decision rights. The task objective and feasible ways to fulfill it become clear together; the user does not need to understand every tool interface first.

## Interactive Negotiation

Suppose the system has tools for querying suppliers, comparing specifications, ordering, paying, and tracking delivery, so the working agent can carry out the purchase. The department already has a recipient responsible for inspecting the physical goods and recording the results; the logistics tool cannot itself confirm what is inside the boxes. These capabilities and coordination arrangements must be considered alongside the current request.

After alignment, the task is specified as follows: purchase five computers for five new colleagues to use for everyday documents and video meetings, meet the department's existing specification standard, keep the total including tax and delivery within CNY 30,000, and have them arrive before Friday. The task includes checking quantity, model, and physical condition, but excludes system installation. These are the agreed requirements in this example.

This judgment involves more than filling a purchase form from a sentence. “Arrive before Friday” and “be ready for the new colleagues to use on Friday” may require different delivery conditions. The governance agent must consider the intended use, installation capabilities, and receiving arrangements to establish which commitment the system is taking on, and refer outstanding tradeoffs to an authorized party. A model cannot simply choose an interpretation and treat it as a commitment the user has accepted.

The work arrangements follow: the working agent queries suppliers, selects models, orders, pays, and tracks delivery; the designated recipient supplies the physical inspection results. The governance agent should confirm that the existing receiving arrangement applies to this task and include those results in the completion evidence. A delivery signature alone cannot establish that the whole task is complete. Where participants, tools, or observation methods are not yet in place, the system should identify which requirements it cannot currently undertake.

Delivery requirements must also reflect what the system can control. The working agent can select an option using a supplier's estimated arrival information, but cannot guarantee that transport will be on time. The mandate must therefore specify which adjustments the governance agent may decide when a delay is expected, and which decisions are left to a person when human involvement is configured. Recognizing uncertainty does not authorize the system to relax the requirement to arrive before Friday.

## Commitment Operationalization

Having a payment tool does not confer permission to make this payment. The governance agent should connect the objects, actions, and data needed for the purchase to existing rules and valid authorization: which suppliers and payment routes may be used, what the budget limit is, for what purposes receiving information may be used, and which transaction conditions must be met.

Suppose autonomous purchasing through a designated supplier directory is authorized, and the budget approval and tool permissions cover the corresponding orders and payments. The applicable purchasing rules allow one deposit and require the seller to confirm that the goods are ready before the balance is paid; these rules will apply to particular transactions during execution. Budget permission does not automatically authorize duplicate payments, and access to personnel data does not permit sending all of it to a supplier. Existing valid authority can be used directly; missing authorization must come from an authorized party.

Governance authority must also be put into effect. In this example, the governance agent may check order and payment status and use execution controls to hold payments whose conditions are unmet. It may not independently raise the budget or change the delivery requirements. Permission to observe does not imply the ability to stop an action; the mandate should correspond to controls the system can actually use.

The resulting mandate brings together task requirements, work assignments, authorization scope, action conditions, and completion evidence, along with applicable requirements for retaining and accessing key records. Requirements and conditions that need to be checked during execution are collectively called governance rules. Mandate Alignment identifies and implements existing rules and, where necessary, obtains new valid decisions; it does not independently create organizational policy or expand authorization. Nor must all these rules be expressed as program code.

Commitments also need concrete implementation: the cost limit connects to order and payment checks, the readiness condition connects to the control that releases the balance payment, and physical requirements are checked by the designated recipient. Fixed programs can check amounts and recipients, but someone must first establish what to check, where the evidence will come from, and who can handle conditions that cannot be determined automatically. Putting “must meet the requirements” in a prompt does not establish those checks and controls.

## Continuous Maintenance

A change in execution state does not always require changing the mandate. A payment moving from unconfirmed to successful updates a fact under existing rules. Changes to quantity, budget authorization, or the availability of capabilities and coordination arrangements require realigning the affected requirements and conditions. The governance agent should adopt valid changes made by authorized parties, identify the subsequent actions they affect, and retain the original mandate and the grounds for the update for later review.

An update cannot stop at the mandate text. Affected working agents, payment checks, and receiving arrangements must adopt the version in effect. Orders or payments already made require separate checks: changing a quantity or budget does not automatically change an existing order. Fixed configuration synchronization can propagate known fields, but it remains necessary to determine which commitments, controls, and actions in progress are affected by a particular change in requirements.

The mandate must also specify governance decision rights and termination authority. When authorized to act autonomously, the governance agent handles changes within its assigned governance authority and makes authorized rule adjustments or execution decisions. If it determines that the task cannot continue under current conditions, it may terminate it and explain the grounds without waiting for a human to take over. With human involvement, a person may revise rules or make a new decision; the decision request, waiting state, and follow-up responsibility should be explicit. Mandate changes should retain their source, version, and scope of effect; overwriting the old mandate must not erase earlier actions and results.
